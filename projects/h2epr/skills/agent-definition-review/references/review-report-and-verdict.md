# Review report and verdict

Write a concise but evidence-rich review. Lead with the substantive result,
not the review process.

## Recommended report structure

### Review identity

Record:

- Definition identity and semantic version;
- reviewed files or stable candidate identity;
- event and participant scope;
- review date and reviewer;
- evidence, source, and access boundary;
- whether comparison Agents were included;
- whether runtime code or output was deliberately excluded.

### Overall judgment

In a short paragraph state:

- what the Definition successfully explains;
- its strongest scholarly or modeling contribution;
- its most serious limitation;
- whether it is ready for a reference candidate.

### Claim and model summary

Summarize the represented actor, institutional position, central mechanisms,
information boundary, focal decisions, intent repertoire, and validity scope.
This confirms that the reviewer understood the proposed model before
criticizing it.

### Strongest counterargument

Present the strongest plausible evidence-based challenge to the representation
or mechanism and explain whether the Definition answers it.

### Findings

For each finding provide:

```text
severity=
location=
claim_or_mechanism=
evidence=
why_it_matters=
required_revision=
return_stage=
rereview_check=
```

Use precise locations and quote sparingly. Do not bury a material evidentiary
or institutional flaw among style notes.

### Cross-section and cross-agent consistency

Report orphan observations, state, parameters, mechanisms, intents, cases, and
falsifiers. When multiple Agents are reviewed, distinguish legitimate
institutional asymmetry from inconsistent methods or evidence standards.

### Unresolved evidence and alternatives

List bounded unknowns, competing mechanisms, inaccessible sources, exposed
outcomes, and assumptions. State whether each can remain in a reference
candidate or blocks it.

### Verdict and revision route

Choose one primary verdict.

## Verdicts

### `READY_FOR_REFERENCE_CANDIDATE`

No blocking or major substantive finding remains. The Definition is
historically and theoretically grounded for its stated use, institutionally
defensible, behaviorally explicit, falsifiable, internally consistent, and
publication-ready. This verdict does not approve runtime implementation or
claim event-level validity.

### `REVISE_AND_REREVIEW`

The representation and research basis remain viable, but one or more major
problems in exposition, mechanism, information, state, parameters, cases,
uncertainty, or consistency require a new Definition candidate.

### `RETURN_TO_BEHAVIOR_RESEARCH`

The document lacks a defensible or sufficiently developed mechanism,
institutional analysis, decision situation, uncertainty model, or falsifier.
Editing the Definition alone would conceal the gap.

### `RETURN_TO_EVIDENCE_RESEARCH`

A material historical, institutional, theoretical, empirical, parameter, or
participant-time claim lacks adequate evidence or uses the evidence beyond its
support.

### `RECONSIDER_PARTICIPANT_REPRESENTATION`

The modeled decision interface aggregates or omits actors, governance,
relationships, or information differences necessary to explain the focal
process. Reframe the participant before further prose refinement.

## Rereview

The rereview should:

- use a new candidate identity or semantic version;
- trace every blocking and major finding to a revision;
- verify that revisions did not create cross-section drift;
- repeat the relevant adversarial checks;
- record unresolved findings rather than silently dropping them;
- avoid expanding scope unless evidence and project authorization also expand.

Minor editorial corrections can be checked together. A representation,
mechanism, evidence-use, information-boundary, or intent/result change requires
a full substantive rereview.
