# Cross-agent and publication review

Compare Agent Definitions to ensure a common scientific standard while
preserving legitimate institutional differences.

## Methodological parity

All reference Definitions should apply the same discipline to:

- event and participant scope;
- claim-level evidence and source quality;
- participant-time information and exposure;
- representation and aggregation;
- governance, authority, resources, and relationships;
- theory-to-event applicability;
- observation, private state, and belief;
- Decision Commitments and intent/result separation;
- parameters, worked cases, uncertainty, and falsification;
- references and limitations.

Parity does not require the same number of sections, observations, parameters,
mechanisms, or actions.

## Legitimate asymmetry

Expect Definitions to differ when participants have different:

- legal or institutional form;
- authority and jurisdiction;
- access to information;
- governance and authorization procedure;
- resource ownership and control;
- temporal horizon and commitments;
- available action or communication routes;
- evidence richness and uncertainty;
- discretionary versus protocol-driven behavior.

Do not make two roles symmetric merely to simplify a runtime interface. Do not
make them artificially different through names, generic risk scores, arbitrary
thresholds, or unequal evidence standards.

## Shared decision-situation comparison

For at least one common event situation, compare:

| Dimension | Questions |
|---|---|
| World position | Which conditions affect each participant? |
| Legal observation | Who sees what, when, through which channel, and at what granularity? |
| Private state | Which prior commitments or procedure states matter? |
| Authority | Which intents are available, prohibited, or conditional? |
| Goals and duties | What must be satisfied before discretion? |
| Mechanism | Why should responses differ? |
| Intent | What may each participant attempt or communicate? |
| Environment result | Which outcome remains outside both participants' control? |
| Prediction | What structural difference should appear in the process? |
| Falsifier | What result would show that the role distinction is decorative? |

## Evidence balance

Compare whether one Definition makes stronger claims from weaker sources, uses
later outcomes more freely, relies on theory summaries instead of original
works, or hides more assumptions. Equal source counts are irrelevant; equal
claim discipline is essential.

Where evidence is asymmetric, the Definitions should show asymmetric certainty
and scope rather than manufacture symmetry.

## Terminology and semantic consistency

Check that shared concepts retain compatible meanings:

- observation, private state, belief, and world state;
- authority, eligibility, authorization, and feasibility;
- action intent, message, delivery, disposition, execution, and result;
- pending, delayed, partial, failed, expired, and cancelled;
- event fact, theory, estimate, analogy, assumption, and uncertainty.

Participant-specific historical terms should remain distinct when their
institutions differ.

## Publication review

Read the set as a prospective paper appendix or model handbook:

- Is the shared method apparent without repetitive boilerplate?
- Does each participant receive enough historical and theoretical context?
- Can readers compare roles through a common conceptual vocabulary?
- Are differences explained causally rather than cosmetically?
- Are citations complete and consistently formatted?
- Do documents avoid internal engineering and status language?
- Are limitations and exposure stated once clearly rather than repeated after
  every paragraph?
- Do the examples collectively demonstrate the method's range?

## Cross-agent verdict questions

- Would exchanging only the participant names leave behavior unchanged?
- Would exchanging institutional position and authority change behavior in the
  predicted way?
- Does either Agent receive forbidden information or broader action power for
  convenience?
- Is one Agent's uncertainty hidden inside the other Agent or scenario?
- Do interactions require an omitted third participant or process?
- Can the shared Definition method express both roles without forcing false
  homogeneity?
- Does success with these roles establish only event-bound expressiveness, as
  opposed to cross-event reuse?
