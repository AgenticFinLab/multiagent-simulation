# Evidence, theory, and provenance

The Definition should make its intellectual and historical basis visible
without becoming a duplicate evidence database.

## Claim use in the Definition

For every material statement, determine whether it is:

- a historical or institutional claim;
- a general theoretical mechanism;
- an empirical regularity or estimate;
- a retrospective interpretation;
- a cross-event analogy;
- an explicit modeling assumption;
- a structural uncertainty or competing proposition;
- an exposed outcome used for illustration or falsification design.

Use ledger claim references for auditability and conventional citations for
scholarly readability. A claim ID is not a substitute for a bibliography; a
bibliography is not a substitute for claim-level time and use control.

## Evidence-to-behavior mapping

For each major mechanism, document:

| Link | Question |
|---|---|
| General basis | Which original theory or empirical source defines the candidate process? |
| Historical applicability | Which event-specific claims show that the relevant institution, information, authority, relation, or condition existed? |
| Translation | What interpretive or modeling step connects the claims to the mechanism? |
| Decision consequence | Which alternatives, precedence, fallback, or intent changes? |
| Observable implication | What process pattern follows if the mechanism matters? |
| Withdrawal | What changes if a supporting claim is disputed or removed? |

Do not cite a broad theory for a precise historical threshold. Do not cite an
observed outcome as evidence that the participant used the proposed mechanism.

## Source and theory standards

- Read the original paper, book, dataset documentation, archival document, or
  authoritative edition used for a publication-facing claim.
- State the population, institution, period, and decision setting to which an
  empirical result applies.
- Explain transfer limits when applying a theory developed for another period
  or institution.
- Trace important retrospective claims to their primary record where feasible.
- Recognize when apparently independent accounts share the same upstream
  source.
- Keep modern institutional analogies visibly separate from historical
  authority or practice.

## Time and exposure

The Definition must respect:

- event time;
- participant-available time and channel;
- source-production time;
- research-access time;
- evidence-use class;
- exposure and held-out status.

Later outcomes can appear in historical context, worked cases, limitations, or
predeclared evaluation design when labeled. They cannot enter the participant's
decision-time observation, private state, or rationale.

If the authors have read the full event outcome, state that exposure plainly.
Do not reconstruct a nominal held-out set from remembered material.

## Competing and unresolved claims

When evidence supports different institutional or behavioral structures:

1. name each proposition;
2. cite its supporting and contradicting claims;
3. explain which decisions differ;
4. preserve explicit alternatives, unknown authority, or sensitivity variants;
5. state what source could resolve the question;
6. do not select the known-outcome-consistent branch merely for narrative fit.

An unresolved question may remain in the canonical Definition if the resulting
behavior and limitations are explicit. It should not be hidden in a confidence
score or unspecified backend discretion.

## Assumptions

For every behaviorally material assumption, state:

- why it is needed;
- which evidence is missing or insufficient;
- which decisions and predictions it affects;
- which alternatives were considered;
- how it can be perturbed or ablated;
- what evidence would revise or remove it.

Avoid assumptions that invent a person, authority, participant-available observation, resource,
historical fact, or later outcome. If the research question cannot proceed
without such an invention, narrow or stop the model.

## Revision responsibility

Record semantic changes such as:

- representation split or aggregation change;
- new or withdrawn evidence claim;
- mechanism added, revised, or rejected;
- observation or private state changed;
- authority or intent boundary changed;
- parameter or uncertainty representation changed;
- worked case or falsifier added;
- limitations or validity scope revised.

Record these changes and their evidence basis in the release or project record
that owns revision history. Stable IDs, semantic versions, source and Definition
hashes, Git commits, generated bindings, and test receipts do not belong in the
public model narrative.
