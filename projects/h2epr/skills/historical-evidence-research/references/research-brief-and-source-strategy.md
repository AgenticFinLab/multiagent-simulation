# Research brief and source strategy

Use this reference before opening new sources. It fixes the question and the
authorized evidence boundary while leaving the search responsive to what the
sources reveal.

## Research brief

Record the following fields in ordinary prose or a table.

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Event | Stable event identity and geographic/institutional setting. |
| Modeled interval | The event period being constructed. |
| Decision time | The time at which the participant is assumed to decide. Use an interval when the exact time is unknown. |
| Participant or institution | The real entity, organizational unit, relation, or decision interface under study. |
| Modeling question | A bounded question whose answer changes representation, information, authority, mechanism, action, parameter, scenario, or falsification. |
| Candidate propositions | Mutually distinguishable answers that the research may support. Include `unknown` where appropriate. |
| Intended uses | Construction, mechanism selection, parameter bounding, scenario, worked case, falsification, or later evaluation. |
| Known exposure | Outcomes, sources, or claims already seen by the model authors. |
| Existing evidence | Local sources and ledger claims already available. |
| Project constraints | Owner decisions and architecture boundaries that control the research but do not count as historical evidence. |
| External boundary | Authorized sites, source classes, dates, cost, credentials, and archival permissions. |
| Exclusions | Reference artifacts, private records, later outcomes, or other material excluded from this run. |
| Stop conditions | Evidence sufficiency, query exhaustion, access boundary, contradiction, time budget, or owner decision required. |

Avoid framing the question around the desired simulation action. For example,
do not ask only for evidence that a clearing house would reject a request. Ask
what authority, eligibility, review, resource, and communication processes
defined the available response set.

## Claim families

Use the families that materially affect the question. Do not fill every family
for completeness.

- legal or institutional identity;
- membership and eligibility;
- representation and corporate authorization;
- governance body and decision procedure;
- resource ownership and control;
- relationship or communication channel;
- participant-time information;
- available action or support route;
- recorded decision or action;
- result, consequence, or later interpretation;
- theoretical behavioral mechanism;
- empirical parameter or state estimate;
- competing historical interpretation.

## Source classes

Source quality is claim-relative. Prefer the closest competent source for the
proposition being tested.

### Institutional and legal sources

- charters, constitutions, bylaws, amendments, resolutions, and circulars;
- minutes, committee records, hearing testimony, and official investigations;
- annual reports, balance sheets, regulatory filings, directories, and
  institutional correspondence.

These sources are usually strongest for formal identity, membership,
authority, procedure, and stated resource rules. They may not reveal informal
practice, private deliberation, or actual compliance.

### Contemporaneous observational sources

- newspapers and financial periodicals;
- public notices, speeches, interviews, and testimony recorded near the event;
- transaction, balance-sheet, membership, market, or administrative records;
- correspondence, diaries, and participant accounts with documented custody.

These sources can establish what was publicly reported or contemporaneously
observed. They require attention to authorship, incentives, reporting delay,
editorial framing, and whether the modeled participant received the same
information.

### Retrospective historical and empirical research

- peer-reviewed history, economics, finance, sociology, and political economy;
- scholarly monographs and critical editions;
- central-bank, archival, or university working papers with source notes;
- datasets reconstructed from documented primary records.

Use these sources for synthesis, reconstruction, competing explanations, and
quantitative estimates. Trace important claims back to cited primary material
when feasible.

### Behavioral and organizational theory

- peer-reviewed theory and empirical studies;
- authoritative books or review articles;
- established formal or qualitative models;
- organization, governance, decision-process, partial-observability, and ABM
  methodology literature.

Theory supports a candidate mechanism or representation. It does not prove
that a named historical participant followed that mechanism.

An advisory report, literature review, or prior synthesis can seed the search.
Use the original cited work for any theory that enters a publication-facing
Definition, and verify that the source supports the mechanism attributed to it.

### Analogy and practitioner material

Modern cases, professional standards, educational material, and informed
practitioner accounts may motivate alternatives or explain terminology. Label
them as analogy or context; do not silently project modern institutions,
central-bank powers, risk systems, or market conventions backward in time.

## Search strategy

### Reactive search

Start with known citations, archive references, official titles, exact names,
dates, statutes, reports, and source notes from already adopted research.

### Proactive search

Build queries across:

- historical and alternate entity names;
- institution + date + action or procedure;
- officer, committee, member, intermediary, counterparty, or relationship;
- source-specific vocabulary used at the time;
- later scholarship's footnotes and bibliography;
- citing and cited sources;
- competing interpretation terms;
- archive, library, government, university, and institutional domains.

Search for disconfirming material deliberately. A source strategy that only
seeks support for the current model cannot adjudicate alternatives.

## Candidate selection

Prioritize a source when it can change at least one of:

- a participant boundary;
- a legal observation;
- an authority or resource relation;
- a behavioral mechanism or competing mechanism;
- a parameter range;
- a scenario rule;
- a worked case or falsifier;
- the classification of a claim as resolved or unresolved.

Deprioritize duplicates, unsourced summaries, generic historical narratives,
and sources that add atmosphere without changing a model claim.

## Stopping strategy

Stop a search branch when one of these conditions holds:

- the claim is supported for its stated use by an appropriate direct source
  and an independent corroborating source where the risk warrants it;
- planned source classes and reference chains have been exhausted without a
  decisive source;
- remaining sources repeat the same upstream account;
- the next step crosses the authorized access boundary;
- the claim remains structurally disputed and further search has diminishing
  value for the current model;
- the question no longer affects an active modeling decision.

Record why the search stopped. “Not found” after one query is not evidence of
absence; “unavailable within the authorized search” is a bounded conclusion.
