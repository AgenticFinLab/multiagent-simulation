# Population model template

Use this template when an event depends on distributed choices but the evidence
does not support, or the question does not require, reconstructing every actor
as a named Agent. A population model represents heterogeneous choice units; it
must not give a group one personality, belief, account, or authority.

All publication-facing population models use the ten modules below in the same
order. This stable reading structure does not prescribe document length. A
standard model may keep a module to a short paragraph or table when that is
enough; a deep model adds detail only where representation, evidence, or causal
risk requires it. The template sets no minimum number of mechanisms, cases,
profiles, parameters, working files, or reviews.

Summarize the event-bound representation and causal role. The overview should
let an external reader distinguish the choice unit from a named Agent, a whole
organization, an aggregate output, and a scenario-owned process.

## 2. Population scope and representation

State what one choice unit represents and why a population is preferable to a
named Agent or a scenario-owned process. Define inclusion, exclusion,
aggregation, host or institution boundaries, and any weights. Name the
evidence or causal finding that would require splitting the population,
promoting a unit to an Agent, or returning the behavior to the scenario.

Do not infer a collective decision-maker from a shared label. Authority,
claims, balances, obligations, and private state remain with the units or their
explicit institutional owners.

## 3. Evidence and theoretical foundation

Reference the adopted source and claim records that support this model. State
the observed regularities, disputes, assumptions, and theoretical mechanisms
that matter to its use. Preserve participant-time and source-use limits; do not
copy the shared participant-evidence record into the model.

Separate historical facts, theoretical mechanisms, estimates, analogies, and
modeling assumptions. Describe uncertainty where the evidence cannot support a
single interpretation.

## 4. Event role and relationships

Explain what the choice units own and how they relate to institutions, Agents,
other populations, and scenario-owned processes. Distinguish information,
authority, resource, claim, request, and result relations where they matter.

State whether several capabilities may belong to the same real institution or
person and how the model avoids duplicating that identity, authority, or
resource state. A relationship permits only the observations and intents
explicitly assigned to it.

## 5. Decision situations, information, and state

Define the situations that activate a choice and what a unit can observe at the
modeled time, including missing, delayed, disputed, or stale information. For
each behaviorally material private state, state its owner, initialization
basis, update rule, and isolation boundary.

If an issued investigation, request, message, or control can remain pending or
later fail, expire, or be superseded, retain a reference and the most recent
observed lifecycle state. This memory must distinguish “never issued” from
“issued but unresolved” without becoming a private copy of the authoritative
result.

Describe only the heterogeneity needed for the event question. Units must not
read one another's hidden state or a later historical outcome.

## 6. Behavioral model

Describe the competing mechanisms, available choices, selection basis,
fallback, abstention, and conditions for revisiting a choice. Connect each
material mechanism to evidence or theory and state where it is expected to
fail.

The model should preserve individual variation while making the aggregate
response interpretable. It need not assign every unit a unique biography or
fixed policy. An activated material situation should have a traceable response
or a named blocker and reopening condition.

## 7. Intent and result boundary

List the domain intents produced by a choice unit and the observations or state
that may influence them. Keep delivery, matching, queues, market or technical
processes, adjudication, resource effects, and realized results with the
scenario unless the roster assigns them elsewhere.

Explain how unit choices may be aggregated for analysis without turning that
aggregation into a new actor. A requested action, an accepted request, and its
realized effect are distinct.

## 8. Operationalization and uncertainty

Describe how scenarios instantiate choice units, role or response types,
weights, qualitative parameters, and initial state. Explain how choices may be
summarized or aggregated while preserving their owner and host. State
admissible ranges or categories only when they have a semantic interpretation.

Identify structural, compositional, measurement, and parameter uncertainty.
Numerical calibration is optional and belongs only to a later research
question that needs it; an uncalibrated model should expose its assumptions and
sensitivity dimensions rather than invent point values.

## 9. Worked cases and falsification

Use a small set of high-information situations to show how information,
heterogeneity, missing data, competing mechanisms, and adverse results change
the model's choices. Label any exposed outcome used to construct a case.

State plausible alternatives and observations that would revise the mechanism,
the heterogeneity model, or the population representation. Each principal case
should name one controlled change and the behavior expected to change or remain
invariant. Add numerical sensitivity only when the stated research use needs
it.

## 10. Limitations and references

State what the model does not explain or support, including relevant limits on
historical reconstruction, prediction, calibration, or transfer. Provide
conventional references and identify the evidence claims on which the model
materially depends.

A standard model may use a concise batch-level review. Use a separate review
record for a deep model only when a material representation, evidence, or
causal judgment needs independent treatment. Event integration is recorded in
the batch interface account rather than appended to the public population
model.

## Completion check

A population model is complete for its stated research use when:

- it follows the ten-module publication structure;
- the evidence question is closed for its stated use;
- the representation preserves meaningful heterogeneity and ownership;
- information, private state, intent, scenario process, and result are
  separated;
- mechanisms, operationalization, uncertainty, alternatives, and revision
  conditions are clear; and
- relations with the shared event model are explicit without inventing
  implementation semantics.

These responsibilities belong in the model, shared evidence record, and
existing interface account; they do not create additional workflow stages or
mandatory public files.
