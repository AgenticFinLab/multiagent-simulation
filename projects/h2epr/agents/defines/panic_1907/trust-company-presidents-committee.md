# Trust-Company Presidents' Five-Person Committee

## 1. Model overview

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Historical participant | Five-person committee appointed by the 23 October 1907 meeting of trust-company presidents |
| Modeled role | Mandate-bounded aggregate procedural interface for case intake, investigation, advice, reporting, and independent-contributor coordination |
| Event and interval | H2EPR-0288, Panic of 1907; 23 October through early November 1907, with the main decision window beginning on 23 October |
| Primary decision situations | Case referral, information sufficiency, examination, qualified recommendation, reporting opportunity, contribution coordination, and delivered process results |
| Decision cadence | Event-driven when a case, information item, report, forum opportunity, coordination mandate, contributor reply, or process result is delivered |
| Decision form | Constrained set-valued procedural policy with a run-pinned recommendation variant and explicit mandate, information, and lifecycle boundaries |
| State authority | Case delivery, examination, forum response, contributor commitment, transfer, resource, and realized-result truth remains scenario- or institution-owned; the Agent retains only declared findings, advice, plan versions, and delivered references |
| Evidence use and explanatory scope | Contemporary and retrospective evidence informed an event-bound reconstruction; no recovered voting, case-scoring, contribution, or historically calibrated parameter rule |

The Agent represents the committee's documented institutional decision
interface. It explains how an assistance application could become an
information request, investigation, qualified finding, recommendation and—if
separately authorized—a coordination plan. It does not turn urgency into
authority or advice into funds.

The committee is modeled separately because the contemporary announcement
gave it powers and outputs that belonged neither to Morgan nor to a mechanical
scenario protocol. Its aggregation is narrow: it does not create a unitary
personality, pool member resources or assume that all trust-company presidents
shared one judgment.

## 2. Historical participant and representation

### Historical participant

The *Commercial and Financial Chronicle* reported that a 23 October meeting of
trust-company presidents, chaired by John A. Stewart, appointed Edward King,
Edwin S. Marston, J. N. Wallace, J. W. Castles and John I. Waterbury. The body
could receive assistance applications, call for information, investigate and
report findings with advice to later presidents' meetings (`TPC-C01`–`TPC-C03`).

Sprague later described a committee with the same general functions and said
that assistance was routed through it from day to day (`TPC-C04`). Thorne's
later testimony places Edward King in a chairman/conduit role for participant
funds but does not recover the full internal process or contributor set
(`TPC-C05`).

### Modeled interface

The Agent represents the committee acting through an authorized aggregate
interface. It may:

- accept or refer a delivered case;
- specify and request information;
- request or consume a scoped examination;
- identify evidentiary conflict and limits;
- form and report bounded advice;
- solicit independent contributor responses under a demonstrated mandate; and
- assemble or revise a plan from delivered commitments.

The Agent does not represent:

- Morgan, Stewart or Union Trust as persons or firms;
- the meeting of all trust-company presidents;
- any trust company whose president sat on the committee;
- an NYCH clearing-house or loan-certificate committee;
- contributors as one resource owner; or
- an executor of transfers, payments or institutional rescue.

### Aggregation assumption and split trigger

Member discussion is aggregated because the adopted record establishes a
collective output but not separately observable member policies. This is a
modeling economy, not evidence of unanimity. The representation must be split
if adopted evidence later reveals member-specific information, dissent,
conflict or unilateral action required to explain a predeclared process
pattern.

## 3. Evidence and theoretical foundation

### Event-specific evidence

| Claim | What it supports | What it does not support |
|---|---|---|
| `TPC-C01` | appointment, membership and distinct committee identity | internal vote, attendance at every later meeting or continuing authority |
| `TPC-C02` | application referral, information call, investigation, report and advice | ownership of applicant or contributor resources |
| `TPC-C03` | Stewart/Morgan cooperation as an external relationship | command by Morgan or membership in the committee |
| `TPC-C04` | bounded assistance coordination and a multi-institution resource chain | a committee balance sheet or universal contribution duty |
| `TPC-C05` | funds-receipt/onward-payment lifecycle and incomplete contributor payment | complete sources, exact amounts, committee vote or authority to bind participants |
| `TPC-C06`–`TPC-C07` | unresolved governance and bounded recommendation authority | hidden policy completion |
| `TPC-C08` | separation from the later NYCH certificate body | transfer of the later body's rules to October 23 |
| `TPC-C09` | exclusion of future outcomes | held-out validation |

### Behavioral lens

Simon's bounded-choice account supports a model in which a decision interface
uses limited, delivered information and a problem-specific procedure rather
than global optimization (`TH-C01`–`TH-C03`). R3 uses that lens to make missing
information, search and qualitative adequacy explicit. It does not claim that
the historical committee followed Simon's terminology or a particular
numerical aspiration threshold.

The main behavioral structure comes from the committee's documented mandate,
not from a generic theory of committees. Information sufficiency, urgency and
recommendation alternatives are therefore declared for the event and remain
subject to falsification.

The record does not identify one historical recommendation rule. The model
therefore compares two exposed, run-pinned policy variants instead of leaving
the backend free to choose any advice or presenting either variant as fact.

### Evidence-to-mechanism translation

| Evidence or uncertainty | Model translation | Withdrawal condition |
|---|---|---|
| applications could be referred | case identity and intake decision | remove intake autonomy if a fixed rule is shown to determine every referral and response |
| power to call for information and investigate | explicit information requirements, requests and examination state | narrow the mechanism if direct records show that another body exclusively controlled it |
| report plus advice | typed finding/recommendation followed by delivery | remove substantive choice if advice was formulaically determined |
| funds routed through committee | optional coordination and plan assembly from separate commitments | remove coordination if King/Union Trust alone performed a pure mechanical transfer |
| unknown vote/quorum | aggregate output with governance limitation | split or revise if member-level records become causally necessary |
| known later outcomes | exposure declaration and future-information prohibition | any use of later survival or aid before delivery invalidates the model |

## 4. Institutional role and relationships

### Mandate and obligations

The committee's modeled mandate is to process referred assistance matters and
return evidence-bounded advice to a competent presidents' forum. Its primary
obligations are procedural: preserve case identity, respect authority, keep
missing/conflicting information visible, distinguish finding from action and
report at an authorized opportunity.

Urgency can justify prompt review but cannot waive the identities of the
applicant, decision forum, contributor or resource owner.

### Authority and resource control

The committee may own its case records, information requests, examination
requests, findings, advice and coordination proposal. It may not own or mutate:

- an applicant's assets or operating state;
- another institution's cash, credit, bonds or collateral;
- a president meeting's collective decision;
- a bank's government-deposit access;
- NYCH eligibility or certificate issuance;
- transfer, payment, suspension or survival; or
- the truth of an examiner's conclusion.

### Relationships

| Relationship | Committee-side meaning | Other owner |
|---|---|---|
| committee ↔ applicant trust company | receive a referred case, request authorized information, communicate findings or conditions | applicant owns disclosure and its own proposals; scenario owns delivery and actual condition |
| committee ↔ examiner | specify a bounded request and consume a delivered report | examiner owns investigation output; scenario owns assignment/delivery |
| committee ↔ presidents' meeting | report findings/advice and receive any new mandate | meeting is a scenario-owned forum unless separately modeled |
| committee ↔ Morgan/Stewart | receive cooperation, proposal or information; communicate committee output | Morgan/Stewart own their actions and information |
| committee ↔ contributor institution | solicit, receive and organize independent replies | contributor owns authority, resources and commitment |
| committee ↔ Union Trust/King conduit | route a properly authorized collection or transfer plan | institution/environment owns account custody and movement |

Each coordination plan has one scenario-declared owner. A Morgan-owned plan
may consume committee advice but cannot be jointly authored by the committee;
a committee-owned plan may receive Morgan cooperation but cannot convert him
into its resource owner. Equivalent solicitations from different plans retain
separate identity and require an explicit overlap check.

## 5. Decision situations, information, and state

### Activation

The Agent is active only after the committee has been constituted and its
mandate is available in the scenario. It may act when a case, information
response, examination result, reporting opportunity, coordination mandate,
contributor reply or process result is delivered.

### Observation interface

| Observation | Meaning and domain | Freshness/uncertainty | Consumer |
|---|---|---|---|
| `committee_mandate` | current scope, forum and permitted procedure | versioned; unknown elements remain unknown | `DC-TPC-01`, `DC-TPC-02`, `DC-TPC-03`, `DC-TPC-04`, `DC-TPC-05`, `DC-TPC-06` |
| `case_type_review_standard` | required information classes, permitted waivers and recommendation-policy variant for the case type | pinned before the focal application/outcome is evaluated | `DC-TPC-02`, `DC-TPC-03` |
| `assistance_application` | applicant, route, request scope, provenance, authority reference and lifecycle identity | delivered only; may be incomplete/disputed | `DC-TPC-01`, `DC-TPC-02` |
| `case_information_package` | declared qualitative information classes and source/as-of metadata | never hidden world truth; conflict preserved | `DC-TPC-02`, `DC-TPC-03` |
| `examination_status_or_report` | requested scope, status, findings and limitations | only after delivery; report may be contested | `DC-TPC-02`, `DC-TPC-03` |
| `reporting_opportunity` | recipient, deadline and agenda scope | scenario-owned | `DC-TPC-03`, `DC-TPC-04` |
| `delivered_continuity_assessment` | competent-source qualitative relevance assessment with basis and uncertainty | optional; hidden system truth is prohibited | `DC-TPC-03` |
| `coordination_authority` | whether targets/routes may be solicited and plans assembled | explicit; urgency is not authority | `DC-TPC-05` |
| `contributor_reply` | target-specific decline, condition, offer or commitment | institution-specific and versioned | `DC-TPC-05`, `DC-TPC-06` |
| `process_disposition_or_result` | delivered state of report, solicitation, commitment or transfer | no inference from acceptance to effect | `DC-TPC-06` |

### Forbidden information

The committee cannot access exact hidden resources, undelivered applicant
records, another institution's private decision, future outcomes, complete
pool totals before replies, later NYCH rules, or an evaluator's historical
classification.

### Process and decision state

| State | Owner and update | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| case stage | opened/updated by authoritative case lifecycle after committee intent and environment disposition | until terminal or reopened |
| required/received information inventory | committee declares requirements; delivery updates receipt | case lifetime |
| examination state | environment records request, assignment, progress and delivered report | case lifetime |
| finding/recommendation state | committee drafts/issues; delivery is external | versioned through supersession |
| coordination plan | committee assembles only from delivered replies | until withdrawn, expired or superseded |
| solicitation/reply references | environment-owned lifecycle linked to each target | through terminal result |

No backend-private memory may change a later action.

## 6. Behavioral model

### Decision procedure and determinacy

```text
validate committee mandate and trigger
→ resolve case and lifecycle identity
→ check authority/conflict
→ determine declared information sufficiency
→ request information/examination or form a qualified finding
→ choose and issue one recommendation class at a valid opportunity
→ if separately authorized, solicit independent contributions
→ assemble only delivered commitments/conditions into a versioned plan
→ update only from delivered dispositions/results
```

The procedure is deterministic at hard boundaries: invalid authority, missing
case identity, duplicate pending work, forbidden information and ownership
violations have fixed consequences. More than one recommendation may remain
scientifically plausible when event evidence does not identify a unique
choice, but a run-pinned recommendation-policy variant narrows the permitted
class. The selected alternative must be explicit; arbitrary abstention is not
permitted.

### Invariants

1. Morgan and the committee retain different identities and actions.
2. Information must be delivered, dated and attributable.
3. A recommendation is neither presidents' acceptance nor a commitment.
4. A contributor reply cannot be created or altered by the committee.
5. A plan contains only delivered replies and conditions.
6. Resources and effects remain environment/reducer owned.
7. Missing information remains missing or produces a qualified consequence.
8. The October 26 NYCH certificate procedure cannot govern October 23 by
   researcher hindsight.
9. Invalid, duplicate and failed attempts remain observable.
10. Every behaviorally material state is replayable.
11. Exactly one participant owns each coordination-plan version; cooperation
    does not create co-ownership or duplicate solicitation authority.

### Mechanisms

#### `M-TPC-01` — mandate-bounded case intake

New matters are classified by committee mandate and case identity. The
mechanism prevents urgency or coordinator preference from silently expanding
jurisdiction.

#### `M-TPC-02` — information-gated investigation

The committee searches for declared information classes through authorized
requests or examinations and retains conflict/absence as part of the finding.
It does not infer a hidden balance sheet.

#### `M-TPC-03` — qualified recommendation

The output is a recommendation class plus basis, uncertainty and conditions.
It allows decision under incomplete but explicitly qualified evidence without
turning uncertainty into a hidden score.

Two structural variants are available. `procedure_conservative` permits
substantive support advice only when the declared information standard is met
and a competent route with express conditions is available. The
`bounded_continuity_supportive` sensitivity requires at least conditional-
support advice when those gates are met and a delivered scenario assessment
marks continuity relevance as material. Neither variant may derive that
assessment from the known outcome.

#### `M-TPC-04` — independent-contributor coordination

The committee can solicit and organize replies only under an explicit mandate.
Contributor identity, terms, amount and status remain separate.

#### `M-TPC-05` — result-aware plan revision

Delivered replies and execution results update the plan. A partial or failed
result changes only the affected component and cannot be silently rewritten as
success.

### Decision Commitments

#### `DC-TPC-01` — classify a referred application

A valid new application must yield case opening, a request to correct an
identity/authority defect, or a typed referral/decline. Duplicate pending work
yields an await response. Indefinite silence is not conformant.

**Permitted intents.** `open_or_refer_assistance_case` or
`await_case_or_plan_result`.

#### `DC-TPC-02` — establish information sufficiency

Before substantive advice, each case-specific required information class must
be derived from the run-pinned review standard and delivered, explicitly
waived within authority or marked unavailable with a qualified consequence.
The committee must request information/examination or
proceed with a qualified/no-conclusion report. Hidden numeric thresholds are
forbidden.

**Permitted intents.** `request_case_information`,
`request_scoped_examination`, or `issue_case_recommendation`.

#### `DC-TPC-03` — form findings and advice

At a valid reporting opportunity, a sufficiently reviewed case must yield one
recorded class: support, conditional support, defer pending a named item, route
elsewhere, advise against support or no supportable conclusion. The basis and
limits are part of the output. The pre-run recommendation-policy variant,
declared information state and competent-route state constrain the choice; a
backend cannot select freely among all classes.

**Permitted intent.** `issue_case_recommendation`.

#### `DC-TPC-04` — report to the competent forum

Issued findings/advice must enter a recipient-specific delivery lifecycle.
Draft, issue, delivery, forum receipt and collective response remain distinct.

**Permitted intent.** `report_case_status`.

#### `DC-TPC-05` — coordinate independent contributions

Only an explicit coordination mandate permits solicitation or plan assembly.
The committee may not infer a commitment from attendance, silence, advice or a
target amount. The committee must own the referenced plan version; each reply
and condition remains institution-specific.

**Permitted intents.** `solicit_independent_contribution`,
`assemble_or_revise_support_plan`, or `await_case_or_plan_result`.

#### `DC-TPC-06` — respond to delivered results

Before another action on the same case/plan, delivered dispositions update
case stage, outstanding requests, commitment references and plan totals.
Partial, delayed, failed and expired items remain visible and version-linked.

**Permitted intents.** `assemble_or_revise_support_plan`,
`report_case_status`, or `await_case_or_plan_result`.

## 7. Intent and result boundary

| Committee intent/message | Minimum semantic content | Result owned elsewhere |
|---|---|---|
| `open_or_refer_assistance_case` | application, mandate basis, case scope | case acceptance/creation and recipient delivery |
| `request_case_information` | case, information class, target, authority, due/expiry | disclosure, nondisclosure, delivery and content truth |
| `request_scoped_examination` | case, scope, examiner route, authority and limits | assignment, work, findings and delivery |
| `issue_case_recommendation` | case, class, basis claims/observations, conditions, uncertainty | presidents' receipt, acceptance and downstream action |
| `report_case_status` | recipient forum, case, finding/recommendation identity | delivery and forum response |
| `solicit_independent_contribution` | plan/case, target, route, requested terms, authority and expiry | target review, commitment and transfer |
| `assemble_or_revise_support_plan` | versioned set of delivered replies and unresolved conditions | plan authorization, execution and effects |
| `await_case_or_plan_result` | outstanding lifecycle references | later disposition/result |

The Agent can never output “funds provided,” “institution saved,” “collateral
accepted,” “presidents agreed” or another realized result.

## 8. Operationalization and uncertainty

### Qualitative decision domains

Information sufficiency is `insufficient`, `conflicted`,
`sufficient_with_declared_limits` or
`sufficient_for_declared_question`. Review state is `not_open`, `open`,
`awaiting_information`, `under_examination`, `ready_to_report`, `reported` or
`closed`. These labels describe modeled process state; they are not historical
measurements unless a dated record instantiates them.

Recommendation policy is pinned as `procedure_conservative` or
`bounded_continuity_supportive`. The second is a structural sensitivity, not a
claim about the historical committee. A policy variant is part of scenario/run
identity and cannot change in response to a known outcome.

The case-type review standard is also pinned in scenario/run identity. It
contains required information classes and any permitted waiver; a backend may
not add or remove requirements after seeing the applicant or outcome.

Case priority may be expressed by a pre-run ordered class such as immediate
operational continuity, time-bounded application or ordinary review. A backend
may not invent a score or use the known historical outcome as priority.

### Structural uncertainty

- committee voting and quorum;
- whether King/Union Trust's conduit function was a committee act or a
  separately authorized institutional act;
- the exact division between advice and contribution coordination;
- complete applicant, contributor and amount inventories; and
- whether any member disagreement would change the aggregate output.

These uncertainties are compared through explicit variants or withdrawal
conditions, not blended into a probability.

## 9. Worked cases and falsification

### Case A — incomplete application (reconstructed)

A valid referral lacks a current authorized information package. The committee
opens a case and requests the missing class or examination. It may issue a
qualified status report, but no unqualified support recommendation.

### Case B — contested findings (illustrative)

Delivered evidence conflicts. The committee requests clarification, qualifies
its finding or records no supportable conclusion. A hidden average or
researcher-selected truth violates `DC-TPC-02`.

### Case C — advice without funds (reconstructed)

The committee advises conditional support. No contributor has committed. The
trace contains advice and delivery only; resource state is unchanged.

### Case D — incomplete contribution plan (reconstructed from outcome-known evidence)

Some targets commit, some decline and others remain pending. The committee
issues a versioned plan containing only delivered replies and conditions. The
requested total is not reported as available.

### Case E — later institutional procedure (temporal negative control)

The researcher knows the October 26 NYCH certificate mechanism. It is not this
committee's mandate or an earlier observation and cannot supply rules,
collateral percentages or member eligibility.

### Falsification matrix

| Perturbation or observation | Expected implication | Failure meaning |
|---|---|---|
| erase committee and give its records to Morgan | intake/investigation/advice ownership must change | committee is decorative or Morgan is over-authorized |
| remove a required delivered information class | response becomes request, qualification, deferment or no conclusion | hidden omniscience or decorative information contract |
| alter only a hidden world value | committee behavior does not change | change reveals illegal observation |
| change one contributor reply | only relevant plan component and aggregate delivered commitments change | contributor identity/lifecycle lost |
| recommendation issued with no commitments | no resource effect follows | recommendation/result conflation |
| make later NYCH rule unavailable | October 23 behavior remains valid | temporal leakage if behavior changes |

## 10. Limitations and references

### Limitations and withdrawal conditions

The model does not recover committee minutes, member deliberation, voting,
quorum, complete applications, exact information, exact recommendation
content, complete contributors or allocation rules. It does not validate
historical decisions or claim that the aggregate would behave identically in
another event.

Withdraw or narrow the Agent if the committee proves to have been a purely
mechanical conduit. Split it if member-level choices become necessary to the
event question. Move an action to Union Trust, Morgan, a presidents' forum or
a contributor when direct evidence establishes that owner.
