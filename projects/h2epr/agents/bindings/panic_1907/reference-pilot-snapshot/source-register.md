# Panic of 1907 Agent Definition source register

The five `P4` records below were adopted during the focused two-role behavior study. Three `NBC-EXT` records
were subsequently adopted for the National Bank of Commerce Definition. Eight earlier records are cited as
`BASE-S01` through `BASE-S08`; their original source IDs and uses are listed at the end of this file. The sealed
local evidence archives retain the adopted bytes and checksums.

Archive identities:

- `h2epr-0288-reference-agent-research-20260820` for `P4-S01`–`P4-S05`;
- `h2epr-0288-agent-definition-gap01-gap02-20260819` for `BASE-S01`–`BASE-S08`;
- `h2epr-0288-nbc-agent-research-20260821` for `NBC-EXT-S01`–`NBC-EXT-S03`;
- `h2epr-0288-nbc-agent-research-20260821-supplement-01` for the original page scan supporting
  `NBC-EXT-S02`.

All event material is used for exploratory construction, mechanism selection, parameter bounding,
worked examples, or falsification design. The historical outcome is already known to the research team, so
none of these sources is labeled held-out evidence.

## `P4-S01` — Sprague, 1910

- Citation: O. M. W. Sprague. *History of Crises Under the National Banking System*. National Monetary
  Commission, Senate Document No. 538, 61st Congress, 2nd Session. Washington: Government Printing
  Office, 1910.
- Class: official commissioned retrospective economic history with reproduced institutional documents.
- Public file: https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/files/docs/historical/nmc/nmc_538_1910.pdf
- Custody: FRASER scan of the National Monetary Commission volume.
- Adopted locations: printed pp. 251–253; Appendix Note J, printed pp. 420–431.
- Adopted for:
  - Knickerbocker scale, clearing-balance pressure, clearing-agent withdrawal, late examination, board
    reassurance, three-hour withdrawal episode, suspension, asset illiquidity, reserve context, and later
    reorganization;
  - the distinction between immediate liquidity and ultimate asset value;
  - the October 26 member loan-certificate procedure as later institutional context only.
- Important contradiction: Sprague states that apparently no proposal to assist Knickerbocker was considered,
  whereas later Wicker-derived accounts record a loan request to NYCH. The source is adopted partly to keep
  this conflict visible.
- Limitations: retrospective; evaluative judgments are the author’s; it does not identify the focal requesters
  or reproduce October 21 NYCH minutes; reported deposit totals may use a different date or definition from
  later reconstructions.
- Archive: `raw/sprague_history_crises_1910.pdf`
- SHA-256: `88f7598816e17557c0d9d4fc52a8336499a34bcbc7e0cb9c6f0cf6200c957fd7`

## `P4-S02` — Cannon, 1910

- Citation: James G. Cannon. *Clearing-House Methods and Practices*. National Monetary Commission,
  Senate Document No. 491, 61st Congress, 2nd Session. Washington: Government Printing Office, 1910.
- Class: institutional/practitioner description prepared for an official commission, with reproduced forms
  and rules.
- Public file: https://elischolar.library.yale.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=13746&context=ypfs-documents
- Custody: Yale Program on Financial Stability scan.
- Adopted locations: printed pp. 159–180, especially pp. 159–162 and 166–178.
- Adopted for:
  - association meetings, voting, officers, manager, standing committees, special loan committees, and
    committee authority;
  - the clearing-house committee’s examination/security power and authority to permit or refuse a member’s
    clearing for an outside institution;
  - nonmember clearing-agent responsibility and notice;
  - trust-company examination, weekly condition statements, officer certification, and the reproduced
    board/trustee/executive-committee authorization form for a clearing relationship.
- Temporal limit: the book describes both historical rules and the 1909 state of the association. Provisions
  enacted after October 1907, especially the January 1908 membership/reserve changes, are not used as 1907
  rules.
- Limitations: it describes general institutional arrangements, not the October 21 request; a generic
  authorization form does not prove which Knickerbocker body authorized the focal request.

## `P4-S04` — Moen and Tallman, 1995

- Citation: Jon R. Moen and Ellis W. Tallman. “Clearinghouse Access and Bank Runs: Comparing New York
  and Chicago During the Panic of 1907.” Federal Reserve Bank of Atlanta Working Paper 95-9, 1995.
- Class: retrospective empirical economic research using reconstructed institution-level balance-sheet data.
- Public file: https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/files/docs/historical/frbatl/wp/frbatl_wp_1995-09.pdf
- Custody: FRASER scan of the Federal Reserve Bank of Atlanta working paper.
- Adopted locations: pp. 5–17, especially pp. 9, 13–16 and Tables 1–3.
- Adopted for:
  - trust-company asset/liability and institutional context;
  - separating liquidity from a capital-based solvency proxy;
  - the association between cash-to-demand-deposit position, clearinghouse access, and deposit contraction;
  - the result that the membership coefficient corresponds to about 20 percentage points less mean demand-
    deposit contraction in the authors’ specification;
  - the need to treat clearinghouse access as a bundle that may include liquidity, monitoring, collective
    guarantee, and depositor signaling rather than a single “rescue” variable.
- Limitations: observational cross-section; membership is represented by a crude institutional-type dummy;
  city and institution effects cannot be completely separated; the capital proxy does not measure anticipated
  portfolio losses; the paper cannot establish an individual participant’s decision rule.
- Archive: `raw/moen_tallman_clearinghouse_access_1995.pdf`
- SHA-256: `008e0559727c8c797515e1aaa1db4944d10249922ee369499f9367b9a5444376`

## `P4-S05` — Simon, 1956

- Citation: Herbert A. Simon. “Rational Choice and the Structure of the Environment.” *Psychological
  Review* 63, no. 2 (1956): 129–138.
- Class: original behavioral decision theory.
- Public file: https://iiif.library.cmu.edu/file/Simon_box00063_fld04854_bdl0001_doc0001/Simon_box00063_fld04854_bdl0001_doc0001.pdf
- Custody: Carnegie Mellon University Libraries scan from the Herbert A. Simon collection.
- Adopted locations: pp. 129–138, especially pp. 129–131 and 136–138.
- Adopted for:
  - a bounded-information, bounded-computation alternative to global optimization;
  - the proposition that useful simplifications depend jointly on the decision interface and its environment;
  - limited-horizon, aspiration, priority, persistence, and clue-guided mechanisms as possible modeling
    families that do not require a single global utility function.
- Limitations: the paper’s illustrative organism is not evidence about 1907 institutions. No numerical value,
  aspiration level, or specific priority is transferred into either participant model.
- Archive: `raw/simon_rational_choice_environment_1956.pdf`
- SHA-256: `6d0c480315ad76e66e4afa4f1b5cf4186e13a4b5f09d3655759a7552133107d8`

- Class: contemporary legal periodical reproducing the enacted statutory text.
