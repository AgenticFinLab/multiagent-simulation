## Research brief

### Question and evidence objective

The accepted question asks how incident and defect signals moved through
corporate investigation and escalation, supplier and replacement-device
handling, carrier and retailer action, and consumer-safety and transport
regulation, and how the timing and authority of the first recall, replacement
response, renewed incidents, and cross-organizational restrictions changed
the process. The evidence objective is correspondingly narrow: establish the
minimum sourced transitions and authority distinctions required to represent
that process without giving 2016 actors later knowledge or forcing the known
outcome.

### Claim families

The research was organized around seven claim families:

1. commercial launch and the initial product population;
2. incident-signal receipt, corporate investigation, and supplier-facing
   inspection;
3. the distinction among a corporate sales stop and replacement program, a
   regulator warning, and a formal jurisdictional recall;
4. replacement-device distribution and the carrier/retailer remedy channel;
5. renewed reports involving replacement devices, renewed investigation, the
   requested global partner stop, and the production halt;
6. the expanded recall and authority-specific transport restrictions; and
7. the 23 January retrospective investigation and remediation announcement,
   quarantined from 2016 participant knowledge.

### Source strategy, selection, and stopping rules

Primary institutional records were preferred because the material questions
concern who had authority to announce, request, approve, recall, restrict, or
halt. Samsung newsroom and investor disclosures support Samsung's own actions
and knowledge claims; CPSC records support U.S. warning and recall states;
CAAC and the U.S. DOT/FAA/PHMSA order support transport authority. Corporate
self-description is not used to prove an engineering mechanism, while a
regulator's incident count remains a dated administrative snapshot rather
than an independently reconstructed incident database.

A source entered the adopted register only if it was opened beyond a search
result, had a stable official locator, disclosed enough context to locate the
claim, and played a distinct role in an atomic claim. Search results and
snippets were discovery aids only. Secondary reporting was unnecessary for
the adopted set: it did not offer a claim role that justified weaker
authority or additional ambiguity. Sources were not selected to maximize
agreement, and apparent conflicts were retained.

Research stopped when each transition in the bounded process had direct
primary support, the corporate-program/formal-recall distinction was closed
by contemporaneous sources from both institutions, the replacement reopening
and final restrictions had authority-specific records, and the remaining
uncertainties could be stated as owner gates rather than repaired with
speculation. Research did not continue into return-rate, market, litigation,
or later design-performance evidence because those claim families are outside
the Frame boundary.

## Conflicts, supersessions, and retrospective findings

### Authority and terminology

Samsung's 2 September sales stop and replacement program is frequently called
a “recall” in later or non-U.S. descriptions, including the background of the
CAAC notice. CPSC's 9 September statement, however, explicitly says that a
formal official U.S. recall was still being prepared, and S06 fixes that
transition on 15 September. The Frame therefore preserves three states:
corporate program, regulator warning/review, and formal jurisdictional recall.
This is an authority distinction, not a claim that one source is false.

### Replacement remedy supersession

The 15 September formal recall allowed a new Note7 with a different battery as
one remedy, and Samsung's U.S. release described that program as CPSC-approved.
The 7 October statement reopened the question after reports involving newly
released replacements. On 13 October the expanded recall covered every
replacement, including green-icon devices. The later action supersedes the
replacement-Note7 remedy as an available safety state; it does not prove that
the earlier decision maker possessed the later reports or diagnosis.

### Incident-count incompatibility

S06 reports, as of 15 September, 92 U.S. overheating reports, 26 burn reports,
and 55 property-damage reports. S11 reports, on 13 October, 96 overheating
reports including 23 new since the first recall, with 13 burn and 47
property-damage reports. The latter burn and property numbers are lower even
though the notice is later. The sources do not explain whether case
definitions, substantiation, deduplication, or reporting windows changed.
These snapshots must not be concatenated or treated as monotonic counters.
Any later use requires an owner-approved incident-data reconciliation; the
Frame uses only the qualitative fact of renewed reports and each notice's
attributed snapshot.

### Request versus implementation

S09 establishes Samsung's request that global carrier and retail partners stop
sales and exchanges. S12 records the CPSC chairman's positive assessment of
wireless carrier and retailer response in the United States. Neither source
supports universal, synchronous, or outlet-level compliance. The Frame
therefore represents intermediaries provisionally as a heterogeneous cohort
and separates receipt, decision, action, and completed remedy.

### Publication-date ambiguity and later diagnosis

S14 carries a U.S. page stamp of 22 January and a Seoul dateline of 23 January.
This likely reflects time-zone or publishing convention, but the evidence does
not require a sharper inference. Both dates lie in the post-crisis observation
layer. The source reports Samsung's retrospective conclusions and remediation;
it does not establish what 2016 actors knew, independently prove physical
cause, assign supplier responsibility, or validate the recall.

No adopted source was silently withdrawn or overwritten in this record. The
material policy supersession is the expanded recall's treatment of
replacement devices, and the unresolved quantitative discrepancy is retained
rather than corrected by the Builder.

## Transport causal ownership and lifecycle semantics

CL06 and CL15 each contain two responsibilities that must remain separate.
First, an authority-specific interface chose whether to issue, withhold, or
escalate a warning or order. That materially autonomous choice is not assigned
to Scenario merely because the adopted evidence closes the historical
issuance result. Second, once an action was validly issued, institutional
processes governed its publication, effective time, routing and delivery,
operator obligations, enforcement, and observable results.

| Authority-specific action | Issuance-choice owner | Downstream lifecycle owner | Entry condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| CAAC 14 September transport warning | **representation gate** for the CAAC warning-issuance decision interface | **scenario or institutional process** for publication, routing and delivery, airline and airport communication obligations, enforcement conditions, and result production | Before production continues for this row, the gate must choose among Agent, population or cohort, and scenario or institutional process, and record why that disposition explains the issuance choice |
| U.S. DOT/FAA/PHMSA emergency order signed 14 October | **representation gate** for the emergency-order issuance decision interface across the authority chain | **scenario or institutional process** for publication, the 15 October effective-time transition, routing and delivery, carrier notification and denial duties, enforcement, and result production | Before production continues for this row, the gate must choose a formal disposition and identify the defensible decision boundary; the existence of the signed order does not itself settle that representation |

These are two separate gates because jurisdiction, authority form, evidence,
and clocks differ. Neither gate admits an Agent or expands a roster. A later
owner may select scenario or institutional-process ownership only by
supplying a concise account of how the material issuance choice is explained
rather than absorbed into the already-issued action's lifecycle.

## Frame evidence-use closure

The adopted evidence now covers every behaviorally material transition used
by the Frame: initial launch; incident-signal and supplier-facing
investigation; the first corporate product action; regulator warning and
formal recall; replacement handling through carriers and retailers; renewed
replacement-device concern; requested stop-sale/exchange, production halt,
and expanded recall; selected transport restrictions; and the bounded
retrospective announcement. Every transition maps to an atomic claim, a
source role, and a four-clock availability statement. CAAC warning issuance
and U.S. emergency-order issuance now have separate causal-owner gates; their
post-issuance publication, effective-time, routing, obligation, enforcement,
and result lifecycles remain institutionally owned.

Closure is deliberately limited. The record does not establish incident-level
truth, supplier culpability, internal corporate knowledge, complete global
coverage, uniform intermediary action, consumer compliance, return rate,
physical defect cause, or any simulation parameter. No later-phase artifact
has been started. Additional research is warranted only if an authorized
owner changes the causal boundary or a later accepted phase demonstrates that
one of the recorded gates is behaviorally necessary.

The Frame readiness invariant is therefore met by bounded gates rather than
premature roster choices. It does not declare a transport-regulator Agent,
does not treat either authority chain as one owner, and does not allow the
scenario-owned lifecycles to absorb the unresolved issuance decisions.

**Frame evidence disposition:** accepted for event framing with bounded
representation gates and recorded limitations. The source set is sufficient
for the accepted question, temporal contract, causal transitions, authority
boundaries, and later roster questions. It does not authorize or supply
participant behavior, parameters, Scenario semantics, runtime work,
simulation, evaluation, or scientific validity claims.
