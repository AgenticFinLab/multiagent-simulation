## Event question and claim boundary

> Within the bounded Galaxy Note7 product-safety crisis, how did incident and
> defect signals move through corporate investigation and escalation,
> supplier and replacement-device handling, carrier and retailer action, and
> consumer-safety and transport regulation to transform a launched product
> into a global stop-sale, production discontinuation, and expanded recall?
> How did the timing and authority of the first recall, replacement response,
> renewed incidents, and cross-organizational restrictions alter that event
> process?

The purpose of the event is to support engineering-and-method mechanism
coverage: it asks how differently situated decision interfaces received
signals, exercised authority, and changed the reachable product-safety
process. The current claim is not calibration, historical fit, held-out
performance, defect-cause proof, liability allocation, recall effectiveness,
scientific validity, predictive accuracy, or universal generality. A source's
report of an incident or investigation finding remains a source-attributed
report; it is not silently promoted into an adjudicated physical cause.

| Post-crisis observation | `2016-10-16_TO_2017-01-23` | Retrospective observation and bounded remediation context only |

Four clocks must remain distinct: the time an event occurred or an order
became effective; the time information could have been available to a
particular participant; the source's publication time; and the research
access time. Public posting does not prove that a participant received or
read a source. Conversely, a later source that recounts an earlier act does
not make the later consolidation available at the earlier date.

The Samsung investigation announcement dated 22 January on the U.S. page and
23 January in its Seoul dateline is post-crisis evidence. Its findings and the
remediation described with them are categorically unavailable to actors in
the 2016 modeled interval. No future participant artifact may expose that
material, the expanded recall, the production halt, or the final transport
ban before the corresponding historical delivery gate.

## Bounded causal scope

The event begins with a commercially launched device population and incoming
incident signals. Those are conditions upon which organizations act, not a
prewritten route to the known outcome.

| Causal class | Included content | Boundary condition |
|---|---|---|
| Initial or exogenous context | Commercial launch in selected markets; devices and battery cells already in circulation; incident reports and physical hazard realizations as they arrive | A report is a signal, not proof of mechanism; device failures must not be generated merely to force the historical outcome |
| Endogenous choices | Samsung investigation, sales/shipment and replacement decisions, supplier-facing inspection, public safety communications, production decision; CPSC warning and recall decisions; the authority-specific choice to issue the CAAC warning and the U.S. DOT/FAA/PHMSA emergency order, each behind its own representation gate; carrier, retailer, consumer, airline, and airport responses when represented | Choice ownership must stay with the historically authorized interface; no later action is written into an earlier choice, and a transport lifecycle may not absorb its unresolved issuance decision |
| Scenario or institutional processes | Signal delivery; jurisdiction-specific recall procedure; replacement inventory and remedy availability; retail and carrier processing; after a transport action is validly issued, its publication, effective-time transition, routing and delivery, carrier or airport obligations, enforcement, and observable result production; physical-device state transitions | These processes may constrain, deliver, or adjudicate choices but may not make an unresolved warning/order issuance choice, impersonate an Agent, or infer undocumented compliance |
| Results | A public corporate sales stop and replacement program; formal U.S. recall; replacement-device reports and renewed investigation; requested global partner stop; production halt; expanded U.S. recall; transport restrictions effective by 15 October | Announcement, legal status, attempted remedy, completed return, and population-level effectiveness remain distinct results |
| Excluded aftermath | Litigation and liability; market-share, financial, brand, environmental, and disposal effects; return-rate claims; post-15 October campaigns; post-crisis design reform beyond bounded retrospective context | These matters cannot be smuggled into the core event as rewards, causes, or known outcomes |

The evidence supports a bounded transition hypothesis: incident signals
prompted corporate investigation and a replacement program; formal consumer
safety authority then changed the U.S. recall state; replacement-device
signals reopened the safety question; corporate, intermediary, recall, and
transport authorities subsequently produced overlapping restrictions through
authority-specific issuance decisions and downstream institutional
lifecycles. This is a sourced process synthesis, not proof that any one action
caused the next or that all jurisdictions and intermediaries behaved
uniformly.

## Shared semantics and ownership skeleton

The following concepts will be interpreted by more than one future product
or have authority that could otherwise become ambiguous. This skeleton fixes
their meanings without entering Scenario or participant design.

| Shared concept | Semantic owner or authority | Minimum invariant |
|---|---|---|
| Incident report / safety signal | Evidence record owns provenance; a future Scenario owns delivery state | `reported`, `received`, `investigated`, and `verified` are different states; arrival time and recipient are explicit |
| Investigation state / finding | The investigating institution owns its internal state; evidence owns public statements | A statement about a finding is attributed and time-gated; the 2017 result is never a 2016 observation |
| Product-flow status | The organization with operational authority owns each state | Sales, shipments, exchanges, production, and completed returns are separate variables rather than one `recall` flag |
| Recall status | Jurisdiction-specific consumer-safety authority plus the initiating firm | Samsung's 2 September program, CPSC's 15 September formal recall, and CPSC's 13 October expansion are distinct states |
| Device class | Product-identity and remedy records | Original device, replacement device, and green-icon replacement are explicit labels; a label is not a safety guarantee |
| Remedy | Firm/intermediary offers; consumer choice; process adjudication | Offer, eligibility, availability, selection, handoff, refund, exchange, and completion remain separable; effectiveness is out of claim scope |
| Restriction-issuance choice | The named authority's decision interface, or a bounded representation gate while that interface remains unresolved | Issuance, withholding, and escalation are autonomous-choice questions distinct from an issued action's lifecycle; CAAC and U.S. transport issuance remain separate gates |
| Issued-restriction lifecycle | Scenario or institutional process after a valid issuance result | Publication, effective time, routing and delivery, carrier or airport obligations, enforcement, and observable results retain issuer and jurisdiction but do not recreate the issuance choice |
| Supplier / battery lineage | Evidence and product-state provenance | Lineage cannot imply cause, knowledge, negligence, or liability; later diagnostic categories are future-only |
| Availability clock | Evidence-delivery boundary | Event time, participant-time availability, source time, and research-access time are recorded separately |
