## §2 Phenomenon Statement

### §2.1 Trigger

A negative shock to the bank-health proxy — realised either as a small exogenous noise draw or as an early duration-loss net-sell impulse from `BondTrader` when yields move against the bank's long-duration book — pushes the proxy price below the intrinsic-health anchor. Once the price-fundamental deviation crosses the `BondTrader` sensitivity band of `abs(deviation) > 0.03`, duration-loss selling becomes active and reinforces the initial move. As deviation deepens past `-0.05`, `SocialMediaInfluencer` panic-amplification activates and converts the visible stress signal into proportional additional sell pressure `min(|deviation| × amplification_factor × 2000, position)`. At this stage `BankManager` support activates on the buy side (`deviation < -0.05` gates a `min(500, floor(cash / price))` support order), but the manager's per-agent cash budget is capped so the support quantity saturates quickly.

### §2.2 Escalation

Sell pressure feeds directly back into the proxy price through the linear price-impact term `price_impact × net_demand` in the market's update law `P(t+1) = max(0.01, P(t) + price_impact × net_demand + mean_reversion × (F − P(t)) + ε(t))`. With `price_impact = 0.025` an order of magnitude larger than `mean_reversion = 0.01`, each round of net-sell imbalance from the panic-amplification and duration-loss channels lifts negative deviation faster than the mean-reversion pull recovers it. Once deviation deepens past the depositor coordination threshold `withdrawal_threshold = 0.1`, all four `Depositor` agents simultaneously flip from `hold` to `sell` and dump up to `min(1000, position)` proxy units per agent per round. This is the coordination-run stage of the Diamond–Dybvig channel, where individual depositor withdrawal becomes rational because other depositors are already withdrawing.

### §2.3 Peak Symptoms

At the peak of the run, negative deviation is deep enough that all sell-side archetypes (`Depositor`, `SocialMediaInfluencer`, and `BondTrader` on the negative-deviation branch) are simultaneously active. Depositor sell orders are typically at or near their per-agent cap of 1000 units. `SocialMediaInfluencer` sell quantities scale with `|deviation|` and are typically in the hundreds. `BankManager` support buying is active but is dominated by aggregate sell pressure because the four-depositor population plus two influencers plus two bond traders on the sell branch outweighs the two-manager buy population. Cumulative depositor exit begins to bind the position constraint: as position runs down toward zero, the `min(1000, position)` cap starts trimming individual orders, but by then the proxy price is already deep in stress territory.

### §2.4 Collapse and Intervention

The collapse mechanism has two exits. The market-driven exit is inventory exhaustion at the depositor archetype: as `position` runs down, individual sell orders shrink, aggregate net demand approaches zero, and the mean-reversion term reasserts itself. The policy-driven exit is `Regulator` intervention: when deviation crosses the severe threshold `intervention_threshold = 0.5`, a Bernoulli draw with probability `guarantee_probability = 0.4` triggers a large 2000-unit buy order. On average one in ~2.5 severe-stress rounds fires an intervention, and the intervention size is calibrated to roughly reverse a single-round depositor cascade. Post-intervention the proxy price recovers toward fundamental and the deviation returns to the band where `Depositor` and `SocialMediaInfluencer` are again inactive.

## §3 Research Goals

1. **Run severity magnitude (parameter sweep).** How does the peak negative deviation scale with `withdrawal_threshold` (Depositor coordination sensitivity) and with `amplification_factor` (SocialMediaInfluencer contagion gain)? Answered by a two-way sweep, measured by `analysis.py: compute_run_severity()`.
2. **Intervention effectiveness (parameter sweep).** How do `intervention_threshold` and `guarantee_probability` jointly determine the fraction of runs that recover before inventory exhaustion? Answered by a two-way sweep, measured by `compute_intervention_success_rate()`.
3. **Amplification ablation (contribution attribution).** How much of the peak negative deviation is contributed by the social-amplification channel? Answered by two single-agent ablations turning off `socialmediainfluencer` and `depositor` in turn, measured by `compute_run_severity()` and `compute_agent_attribution()`.
4. **Rule versus LLM decision fidelity (variant comparison).** Does the LLM variant of the depositor and influencer agents produce systematically different sell quantities than the rule baseline when both face the same proxy-price trajectory? Answered by comparing `Rule` and `LLM` variants declared in §10.1 on `compute_withdrawal_pressure()` and `compute_amplification_pressure()`.
5. **Historical retrieval effect (Rag variant).** Does exposure to retrieved historical bank-run context (SVB 2023, Northern Rock 2007, Continental Illinois 1984, Washington Mutual 2008) change depositor exit timing or regulator intervention framing? Answered by comparing `LLM` and `Rag` variants on peak deviation, `compute_intervention_success_rate()`, and retrieval coverage reported in `rag_stats.json`.

## §8 Environment Specification

### §8.1 Price Formation

Single-clearing-price rule-based coordinator implemented by `Market` in `examples/SVBBankRun/Rule/players.py`. The update law is

`P(t+1) = max(0.01, P(t) + price_impact × net_demand + mean_reversion × (fundamental − P(t)) + ε(t))`

where `ε(t) ~ N(0, noise_std)`, `net_demand = total_buy − total_sell` computed from the current round's `{"action", "quantity"}` orders, `fundamental` is the constant bank-health anchor, and `price_impact`, `mean_reversion`, and `noise_std` are seeded from `market.extras` in `configs/SVBBankRun/*/players.yml`. Justification: §4.1 (Diamond and Dybvig) and §4.4 (Bagehot) as a reduced-form encoding of coordination-run pressure with a mean-reverting fundamental-health anchor and a bounded stabilising channel.

### §8.2 Information Broadcast

Each round the `Market` `decide` step broadcasts a dictionary with six fields to every investor: `price` (current `P(t)`), `fundamental` (constant anchor `F`), `deviation` (`(price − fundamental) / fundamental`, or zero if `fundamental ≤ 0`), `round` (integer index), `volume` (a synthetic clearing-volume statistic `min(total_buy, total_sell) + abs(net_demand) × 0.5`), and `net_demand` (`total_buy − total_sell`). No investor-private fields, no order-book state, and no historical time series are broadcast; agents maintain any needed history in their own `custom_state`. Justification: §4.3 (Bikhchandani et al.) since a single observed public statistic (deviation) plays the role of the observed crowd action that drives the social-amplification channel.

### §8.3 Constraints And Frictions

Short selling: No — sell quantities are capped at current position (`min(quantity, position)`). Cash constraint: Yes — buy quantities are capped at `int(cash / price)`. Margin requirement: No — the reduced-form price-impact rule substitutes for an explicit funding channel. Circuit breakers: No — the anchor episode's collapse was sustained across all four historical cases. Trading hours: No; every round is a full price-formation event. Order type: current-market quantity orders only; the `agent_type` tag on each order is preserved by the `Market` aggregator for accounting.

### §8.4 Round Structure And Granularity

Each round proceeds in three coordinator steps: (1) `Market.perceive` ingests investor orders from the previous round; (2) `Market.decide` computes `net_demand`, updates `price`, computes `deviation`, and broadcasts the market-data dictionary; (3) investors run `perceive → decide → act` and submit fresh `{"action", "quantity", "agent_type"}` orders. One round represents one trading interval in the March 2023 SVB anchor timeline at a granularity coarse enough to capture aggregated hourly behaviour rather than individual withdrawal requests. A 200-round run notionally corresponds to the multi-day run window from the March 8 disclosure through the FDIC receivership and immediate post-intervention tail.
