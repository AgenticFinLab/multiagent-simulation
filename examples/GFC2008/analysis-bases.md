## §2 Core Metrics Catalogue

### Metric: Bubble Build Index (BBI)

#### Category
Price Dynamics / Bubble Phase

#### Definition
The mean positive price deviation from fundamental during the bubble phase (rounds where deviation > 0), measuring the average magnitude of overvaluation generated by the MBSOriginator supply + RatingAgency demand interaction before the crash begins.

#### Formula
```
BBI = mean(dev(t) for t where dev(t) > 0)

where dev(t) = (P(t) − F) / F
```

**Computation notes**: If no positive deviation rounds exist, BBI = 0. Computed from `dev_history` list.

**Python function**:
```python
def bubble_build_index(dev_history: list) -> float:
    """Mean positive deviation during bubble phase.

    Args:
        dev_history: deviation(t) per round
    Returns:
        BBI ≥ 0; higher = larger sustained overvaluation
    """
```

#### Interpretation

| Range        | Economic Meaning   | Simulation Interpretation                              |
|--------------|--------------------|--------------------------------------------------------|
| = 0          | No bubble phase    | RatingAgency or MBSOriginator not activating correctly |
| (0, 0.05)    | Mild overvaluation | overrating_bias low; bubble quickly corrected          |
| [0.05, 0.20] | Moderate bubble    | Consistent with CDO bubble overvaluation 10–30%        |
| > 0.20       | Extreme bubble     | Rating inflation too high; reduce overrating_bias      |

#### Academic Basis

**Primary source**: Bolton, P., Freixas, X. & Shapiro, J. (2012). "The Credit Ratings Game." *Journal of Finance*, 67(1), 85–111.

Rating inflation of 15–30% of fundamental (Gorton, 2010; Pagano & Volpin, 2012) maps directly to BBI ≈ 0.05–0.20 in simulation.

**Supporting studies**:

| Study                             | Context             | Finding                                          | Relevance                |
|-----------------------------------|---------------------|--------------------------------------------------|--------------------------|
| Pagano & Volpin (2012). *RFS*     | CDO ratings 2000–07 | CDOs overvalued 15–30% vs. realized loss rates   | Sets BBI expected range  |
| Griffin & Tang (2012). *JF* 67(4) | Moody's CDO ratings | 2–3 notch inflation ≈ 15–25% price overvaluation | Confirms BBI ≈ 0.05–0.20 |

#### Normal Range
BBI of 0.05–0.20 expected; overrating_bias = 0.20 → price sustained ≈ 15–18% above F during bubble phase.

#### Red Flag Threshold
- **Too high** (> 0.30): overrating_bias too large; reduce to ≤ 0.25
- **= 0**: RatingAgency never activated; check price < perceived_fundamental × 0.95 condition

#### Relationship to Other Metrics
BBI and CII (Crisis Intensity Index) form a pair: BBI measures the height the price was before the crash; CII measures how far it fell below fundamental. A deep crash (high CII) following a large bubble (high BBI) is the expected pattern — simulating the full GFC arc.

### Metric: Crisis Intensity Index (CII)

#### Category
Price Dynamics / Crisis Phase

#### Definition
The maximum negative price deviation from fundamental (maximum depth of the crash), measuring how far below fundamental the fire-sale cascade drives the price at its worst.

#### Formula
```
CII = abs(min(dev(t) for all t))

where dev(t) = (P(t) − F) / F
```

**Python function**:
```python
def crisis_intensity_index(dev_history: list) -> float:
    """Maximum negative deviation magnitude (crash depth).

    Args:
        dev_history: deviation(t) per round
    Returns:
        CII ≥ 0; higher = deeper crash
    """
```

#### Interpretation

| Range        | Economic Meaning | Simulation Interpretation                           |
|--------------|------------------|-----------------------------------------------------|
| < 0.05       | Mild crisis      | Fire-sale never triggers or quickly absorbed        |
| [0.05, 0.20] | Moderate crisis  | LeveragedInvestor activates; partial cascade        |
| [0.20, 0.50] | Severe crisis    | Full fire-sale cascade; DistressedBuyer overwhelmed |
| > 0.50       | Extreme crisis   | Simulation approximation of systemic collapse       |

#### Academic Basis

**Primary source**: Brunnermeier, M.K. (2009). "Deciphering the Liquidity and Credit Crunch 2007–2008." *Journal of Economic Perspectives*, 23(1), 77–100.

S&P 500 fell 57% from peak in GFC; MBS prices fell 30–60% from par. CII ≈ 0.30–0.57 is the empirical benchmark for a full GFC-equivalent crisis in simulation.

**Supporting studies**:

| Study                                 | Context                      | Finding                                                  | Relevance                                          |
|---------------------------------------|------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------|
| Adrian & Shin (2010). *JFI* 19(3)     | Investment bank deleveraging | 20–40% below fundamental during forced deleveraging      | Sets CII realistic range for calibrated simulation |
| Brunnermeier & Pedersen (2009). *RFS* | Margin spiral theory         | Price can fall 20–40% below fundamental in margin spiral | Confirms CII = 0.20–0.40 is realistic              |

#### Normal Range
CII of 0.10–0.40 in calibrated simulation; full GFC analog → CII ≈ 0.30–0.57.

#### Red Flag Threshold
- **Too high** (> 0.70): Fire-sale cascade is runaway; reduce λ or increase DistressedBuyer/Regulator capacity
- **Too low** (< 0.05): LeveragedInvestor never fires; check margin_call_trigger threshold

#### Relationship to Other Metrics
CII is the peak of the crash; FSI (Fire-Sale Index) measures the velocity. High CII combined with high FSI indicates a fast, deep crash (Lehman-analog); high CII with low FSI indicates slow grinding deterioration.

### Metric: Fire-Sale Index (FSI)

#### Category
Agent Activity / Crisis Mechanism

#### Definition
The total number of shares sold by LeveragedInvestor (§4.3) via fire-sale (in rounds where deviation < −margin_call_trigger), divided by its initial position, measuring the fraction of the original leveraged position that was forcibly liquidated by the cascade.

#### Formula
```
FSI = Σ_{t: dev(t) < -trigger} sell_qty_{§4.3}(t) / initial_position_{§4.3}

where trigger = margin_call_trigger parameter
```

**Python function**:
```python
def fire_sale_index(lev_sell_history: list, initial_position: float, trigger: float,
                    dev_history: list) -> float:
    """Fraction of LeveragedInvestor initial position fire-sold below margin trigger.

    Args:
        lev_sell_history: sell quantity of §4.3 per round (0 if not selling)
        initial_position: §4.3 starting position
        trigger: margin_call_trigger threshold
        dev_history: deviation(t) per round
    Returns:
        FSI in [0, 1]; FSI = 1.0 means entire position was fire-sold
    """
```

#### Interpretation

| Range        | Economic Meaning       | Simulation Interpretation                |
|--------------|------------------------|------------------------------------------|
| < 0.10       | Mild fire sale         | Small cascade; quickly absorbed          |
| [0.10, 0.40] | Moderate fire sale     | Significant deleveraging                 |
| [0.40, 0.80] | Severe fire sale       | Most of position liquidated under crisis |
| > 0.80       | Near-total liquidation | LeveragedInvestor essentially wiped out  |

#### Academic Basis

**Primary source**: Adrian, T. & Shin, H.S. (2010). "Liquidity and Leverage." *Journal of Financial Intermediation*, 19(3), 418–437.

Investment banks deleveraged 40–80% of their asset positions during the GFC. FSI of 0.40–0.80 is empirically consistent with GFC deleveraging magnitude.

**Supporting studies**:

| Study                                          | Context             | Finding                                                                        | Relevance                                                        |
|------------------------------------------------|---------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Brunnermeier & Pedersen (2009). *RFS*          | Margin spiral       | Each round of forced selling amplifies the next; geometric decline             | Validates 50% fire-sale rate leading to FSI ≈ 0.75 over 3 rounds |
| Gorton (2010). *Slapped by the Invisible Hand* | Repo market 2007–08 | Haircuts rose from 0% to 25–45% in 12 months; equivalent to forced asset sales | Sets FSI time trajectory                                         |

#### Normal Range
FSI of 0.30–0.80 expected; margin_call_trigger = 0.10 and initial deviation −15% → FSI ≈ 0.50–0.75 over 3 cascade rounds.

#### Red Flag Threshold
- **FSI = 0**: LeveragedInvestor never activates; margin_call_trigger too high relative to achievable deviation
- **FSI = 1 in 1 round**: Fire sale rate too fast; check position arithmetic

### Metric: Recovery Resistance Index (RRI)

#### Category
Behavioral / Stabilization Effectiveness

#### Definition
The fraction of crisis-phase (deviation < −discount_threshold) rounds where DistressedBuyer or Regulator successfully reduced the magnitude of deviation within the next 3 rounds, measuring combined stabilizer effectiveness.

#### Formula
```
RRI = |{t: dev(t) < -d and (|dev(t+3)| < |dev(t)| × 0.70)}| / |{t: dev(t) < -d}|

where d = discount_threshold
```

**Python function**:
```python
def recovery_resistance_index(dev_history: list, discount_threshold: float = 0.15,
                               lookahead: int = 3) -> float:
    """Fraction of deep-discount rounds where deviation reduces 30% within 3 rounds.

    Args:
        dev_history: deviation(t) per round
        discount_threshold: minimum negative deviation to define crisis rounds
        lookahead: rounds to look ahead for recovery
    Returns:
        RRI in [0, 1]; higher = more effective stabilization
    """
```

#### Interpretation

| Range        | Economic Meaning                   | Simulation Interpretation                             |
|--------------|------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------|
| < 0.10       | Stabilizers completely ineffective | Fire-sale overwhelms DistressedBuyer and Regulator    |
| [0.10, 0.35] | Weak stabilization                 | Partial but slow recovery                             |
| [0.35, 0.65] | Moderate stabilization             | Realistic mixed recovery                              |
| > 0.65       | Strong stabilization               | Distressed buying + regulatory intervention effective |

#### Academic Basis

**Primary source**: Bernanke, B.S. (2015). *The Courage to Act: A Memoir of a Crisis and Its Aftermath*. W.W. Norton.

TARP + Fed QE eventually stabilized markets, but the process took 6–18 months. RRI ≈ 0.30–0.55 is consistent with partial, delayed stabilization.

**Supporting studies**:

| Study                              | Context                         | Finding                                                           | Relevance                                                          |
|------------------------------------|---------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Griffin & Xu (2009). *JF* 64(6)    | Hedge fund distressed investing | Smart money (distressed buyers) earned 10–15% returns post-crisis | Validates DistressedBuyer (§4.4) as partially effective stabilizer |
| Acharya et al. (2009). *AER* 99(2) | TARP evaluation                 | TARP succeeded in preventing bank failures but slow recovery      | Sets RRI ≈ 0.30–0.50 for Regulator probabilistic intervention      |

#### Normal Range
RRI of 0.20–0.55 expected; `rescue_probability = 0.60` implies the Regulator activates in most eligible extreme-stress rounds, while `rescue_size = 500` keeps each intervention bounded.

#### Red Flag Threshold
- **RRI = 0**: No stabilization ever succeeds; fire-sale speed too high for recovery within 3 rounds
- **RRI > 0.80**: Stabilizers too powerful; DistressedBuyer or Regulator capacity too large

### Metric: Origination Supply Pressure (OSP)

#### Category
Agent Activity / Bubble Mechanism

#### Definition
The total shares sold by MBSOriginator (§4.1) as a fraction of its initial position, measuring how much of the original MBS inventory is distributed before the crash. High OSP means most inventory was sold during the bubble phase; low OSP means origination was cut short by the crash.

#### Formula
```
OSP = total_sold_{§4.1} / initial_position_{§4.1}
    = (initial_pos − final_pos_{§4.1}) / initial_position_{§4.1}
```

**Python function**:
```python
def origination_supply_pressure(initial_pos_mbs: float, final_pos_mbs: float) -> float:
    """Fraction of MBSOriginator initial position distributed.

    Args:
        initial_pos_mbs: §4.1 starting position
        final_pos_mbs: §4.1 ending position
    Returns:
        OSP in [0, 1]; OSP = 1.0 means fully distributed
    """
```

#### Interpretation

| Range        | Economic Meaning     | Simulation Interpretation                                 |
|--------------|----------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------|
| < 0.30       | Low origination      | MBS supply limited; bubble shorter                        |
| [0.30, 0.70] | Moderate origination | Calibrated range for multi-round simulation               |
| > 0.70       | High origination     | Most MBS distributed before crash; maximum bubble buildup |

#### Normal Range
OSP is nonlinear because quantity is computed from remaining position. With `origination_rate = 0.08`, the originator distributes most inventory over a long full-round run while each round's sell quantity decays as inventory falls.

#### Red Flag Threshold
- **OSP = 0**: MBSOriginator never sells; position always = 0 → check initialization

### Metric: Wealth Distribution Index (WDI)

#### Category
Portfolio

#### Definition
The Gini coefficient of final agent wealth across all 5 agents, measuring inequality in the simulation outcome. In GFC context, expectation is that LeveragedInvestor loses most wealth (fire sale) while DistressedBuyer and Regulator gain.

#### Formula
```
WDI = Gini({W_i})   where W_i = cash_i + position_i × P(T)
```

**Python function**:
```python
def wealth_distribution_index(agent_wealth: list) -> float:
    """Gini coefficient of agent wealth distribution.

    Args:
        agent_wealth: List of final wealth W_i for each agent
    Returns:
        WDI in [0, 1]
    """
```

#### Normal Range
WDI of 0.20–0.50 expected; LeveragedInvestor loss (fire sale at deeply depressed prices) should dominate inequality.

## §3 Analysis Dimensions

### Dimension 1: Bubble-to-Crash Cycle

**Purpose**: Visualize the full two-phase price trajectory — bubble buildup and crash cascade.

**Metrics Used**: BBI, CII
**Visualization**: Line plot — price vs. fundamental across all rounds; shaded positive-deviation (bubble) and negative-deviation (crash) regions
**Expected Pattern**: Price rises moderately above F in early rounds (bubble phase), then crashes sharply below F when LeveragedInvestor fire-sale begins

### Dimension 2: Fire-Sale Cascade Attribution

**Purpose**: Track how LeveragedInvestor's forced selling amplifies the crash.

**Metrics Used**: FSI, CII
**Visualization**: §4.3 position over time (rapidly declining in cascade rounds); overlay with price
**Expected Pattern**: §4.3 position drops geometrically (50% per activation round); each decline further deepens CII

### Dimension 3: Stabilizer Effectiveness

**Purpose**: Measure how effectively DistressedBuyer and Regulator limit crash depth and duration.

**Metrics Used**: RRI
**Visualization**: §4.4 and §4.5 buy volume over time; deviation with and without stabilizers (hypothetical comparison)
**Expected Pattern**: RRI ≈ 0.30–0.55; stabilizers slow but do not prevent cascade

### Dimension 4: Origination and Rating Bubble Dynamics

**Purpose**: Understand the bubble mechanism — how MBSOriginator supply is absorbed by RatingAgency demand.

**Metrics Used**: BBI, OSP
**Visualization**: §4.1 sell volume vs. §4.2 buy volume per round; price deviation overlay
**Expected Pattern**: §4.2 absorbs most of §4.1 supply in bubble phase; bubble ends when §4.2 cash is depleted

### Dimension 5: Wealth Redistribution

**Purpose**: Document the wealth transfer from LeveragedInvestor (fire-sale losses) to DistressedBuyer (discount purchases).

**Metrics Used**: WDI
**Visualization**: Bar chart of final wealth by agent type

## §4 Phase Analysis Framework

| Phase | Name                  | Entry Condition            | Exit Condition                        | Key Indicators                                                      |
|-------|-----------------------|----------------------------|---------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1     | Bubble Build          | Round 1; dev > 0 initially | dev crosses 0 downward                | §4.1 selling; §4.2 absorbing; BBI accumulating                      |
| 2     | Transition            | dev crosses 0              | dev < −margin_call_trigger            | Noise or cumulative selling pushes price below F; §4.2 still buying |
| 3     | Fire-Sale Cascade     | dev < −margin_call_trigger | §4.3 position exhausted               | §4.3 cascade; FSI accumulating; CII deepening                       |
| 4     | Stabilization Attempt | §4.4 and §4.5 activating   | dev returns above −discount_threshold | RRI events accumulating; slow recovery                              |
| 5     | Post-Crisis           | dev > −discount_threshold  | End of simulation                     | Mean-reversion dominant; slow price recovery                        |
