## §2 Phenomenon Statement

### §2.1 Trigger

A modest positive deviation of market price from the intrinsic-value anchor — seeded by exogenous noise or by an early buy imbalance from noise traders — pushes the price-fundamental deviation above the trend-activation threshold `abs(deviation) > 0.02`. Once that threshold is crossed on the upside, TrendChaser and SocialProofFollower agents flip from `hold` to `buy` and begin scaling their orders with the magnitude of the deviation itself: `quantity = min(800, int(abs(deviation) * 5000))`. Because the intrinsic-value and early-exit correction thresholds sit higher at `0.05`, there is a "no-man's-land" band between roughly 2% and 5% premium where only positive-feedback demand is active, letting the deviation ratchet upward with no counterweight beyond weak mean reversion and stochastic noise.

### §2.2 Escalation

Positive-feedback buying feeds directly back into the price equation `P(t+1) = P(t) + price_impact * NetDemand + mean_reversion * (fundamental - P(t)) + epsilon` through the `price_impact = 0.02` gain, so every net-buy imbalance from trend and social-proof agents lifts price further above fundamental. Rising price expands the deviation, which by the linear-in-deviation quantity rule expands next-round trend-follower and social-proof order size, which lifts price again. The stabilising `mean_reversion = 0.008` term is an order of magnitude smaller than `price_impact`, so during the ratcheting phase it recovers only a small fraction of each round's positive-feedback impulse. Social-proof demand runs on the same signal as trend demand but represents a distinct behavioural channel (conformity rather than technical momentum), so the two amplifiers act in parallel and together saturate at 800 units per agent per round only when deviation exceeds roughly 16%.

### §2.3 Peak Symptoms

At the top of the mania, market price sits materially above the intrinsic anchor — commonly in the 20% to 50% premium range under default parameters, with tails permitted by the noise term. Both amplifier archetypes are typically saturated at their per-round quantity caps. IntrinsicValueTrader and EarlyExitTrader are both active on the sell side because deviation exceeds their common 5% activation threshold; their combined selling pressure begins to offset amplifier buying but does not yet dominate because they draw on smaller aggregate inventory (two instances each) than the combined trend and social-proof population (seven instances). NoiseTrader flow is unchanged in expected magnitude but its price impact is largest at the peak in relative terms because the deviation is most sensitive to the sign of net demand when amplifiers are near saturation.

### §2.4 Collapse

The collapse mechanism is symmetric to the ignition mechanism. When cumulative selling from IntrinsicValueTrader and EarlyExitTrader agents plus the mean-reversion term causes any single-round net demand to turn negative, the price impact term flips sign and price falls. A small negative deviation is enough to disarm the trend and social-proof buy leg (which requires positive deviation) while the value/exit sell leg remains active until deviation falls below the 5% band. Once deviation crosses zero, trend and social-proof agents reactivate on the sell side (their rule sells on negative deviation with the same quantity formula), producing a positive-feedback crash that mirrors the ignition. The cascade halts when position inventory is exhausted at the amplifier archetypes, mean reversion pulls the residual deviation back toward zero, and the system settles into a stochastic band around the intrinsic anchor.

## §3 Research Goals

1. **Bubble premium magnitude (parameter sweep).** How does the peak bubble premium scale with `price_impact` and with the trend/social-proof activation threshold (`0.02` in the Rule baseline)? Answered by a two-way sweep, measured by `analysis.py: compute_bubble_premium()`.
2. **Amplifier ablation (contribution attribution).** How much of the peak premium is contributed by TrendChaser versus SocialProofFollower? Answered by two single-agent ablations turning off `trendchaser` and `socialprooffollower` in turn, measured by `compute_bubble_premium()` and `compute_agent_attribution()`.
3. **Correction-force calibration (parameter sweep).** How does the intrinsic-value and early-exit activation threshold (`0.05` in the Rule baseline) affect crash timing and depth? Answered by a sweep on the shared correction threshold, measured by `compute_early_exit_timing()` and `compute_crash_magnitude()`.
4. **Rule versus LLM decision fidelity (variant comparison).** Does the LLM variant of trend and social-proof agents produce systematically different order quantities than the rule baseline when both face the same price trajectory? Answered by comparing `Rule` and `LLM` variants declared in §10.1 on `compute_trend_chasing_demand()` and `compute_social_proof_demand()`.
5. **Historical retrieval effect (Rag variant).** Does exposure to retrieved Dutch tulipmania and later-bubble context change amplifier urgency or exit timing? Answered by comparing `LLM` and `Rag` variants on peak premium, `compute_early_exit_timing()`, and retrieval coverage reported in `rag_stats.json`.

## §8 Environment Specification

### §8.1 Price Formation

Single-clearing-price rule-based coordinator implemented by `Market` in `examples/TulipMania/Rule/players.py`. The update law is

`P(t+1) = max(P(t) + price_impact * NetDemand + mean_reversion * (fundamental - P(t)) + epsilon, 0.01)`

where `epsilon ~ N(0, noise_std)`, `NetDemand = total_buy − total_sell` computed from the current round's `{"action", "quantity"}` orders, `fundamental` is the constant intrinsic-value anchor, and `price_impact`, `mean_reversion`, and `noise_std` are seeded from `market.extras` in `configs/TulipMania/*/players.yml`. The strictly positive floor `0.01` preserves divisibility. Justification: §4.1 (Shiller) and §4.4 (Minsky) as a reduced-form encoding of positive-feedback dynamics with a weak fundamental anchor.

### §8.2 Information Broadcast

Each round the `Market` `decide` step broadcasts a dictionary with four fields to every investor: `price` (current `P(t)`), `fundamental` (constant anchor `F`), `deviation` (`(price − fundamental) / fundamental`, or zero if `fundamental ≤ 0`), and `round` (integer index). No investor-private fields, no order-book state, and no historical time series are broadcast; agents that need history maintain it in their own `custom_state`. Justification: §4.5 (Bikhchandani et al.) since a single observed public statistic (deviation) plays the role of the observed crowd action that drives the herding channel.

### §8.3 Constraints And Frictions

Short selling: No — sell quantities are capped at current position. Cash constraint: Yes — buy quantities are capped at `int(cash / price)`. Margin requirement: No — the reduced-form price-impact rule substitutes for an explicit margin channel. Circuit breakers: No — the anchor episode's collapse was sustained. Trading hours: No; every round is a full price-formation event. Order type: current-market quantity orders only; `bid_price` and other limit-order fields are ignored by the `Market` aggregator.

### §8.4 Round Structure And Granularity

Each round proceeds in three coordinator steps: (1) `Market.perceive` ingests investor orders from the previous round and updates `price`; (2) `Market.decide` composes and broadcasts the market-data dictionary; (3) investors run `perceive → decide → act` and submit fresh `{"action", "quantity"}` orders. One round represents one trading interval in the 1636–1637 anchor timeline at a granularity coarse enough to capture aggregated tavern-college session behaviour rather than individual bilateral trades. A 200-round run notionally corresponds to the roughly three-month peak window from mid-November 1636 through early February 1637 followed by the immediate post-collapse tail.
